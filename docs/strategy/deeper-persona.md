### Looking at the Converse: The Awful Customer

The worst customer I can imagine:

**"Just tell me which AI tool to buy."** They want a product recommendation, not a methodology. They'll take the boundary map and skip the reasoning. They'll implement the one-page policy and never update it. They'll tell their colleagues "this AI thing is sorted" and stop thinking about it.

**"Can you just do it for us?"** They want us to run their AI, not teach them how to govern it. They're looking for an outsourced service, not a capability. They'll become dependent rather than empowered, and they'll blame us when something goes wrong that they should have been overseeing.

**"We need something enterprise-grade."** A 200-person firm that wants a custom implementation and a dedicated account manager. They'll consume enormous amounts of time in sales cycles, demand features that don't exist yet, and negotiate on price. They're not our market — they have in-house teams and consultants for this.

**"AI is going to replace lawyers anyway."** They've already decided the answer before we start. They don't want boundaries — they want confirmation that AI can do everything. Our methodology frustrates them because it says "no, not everything" and they don't want to hear that.

**"We tried AI and it didn't work, so we're done."** The door is closed. They've formed a permanent opinion from one bad experience and they're not open to a different approach. Every conversation is an argument you have to win, and even if you win the argument, they won't act.

**Personality characteristics of the awful customer:**
- Wants conclusions, not reasoning
- Delegates AI decisions to someone else (junior staff, IT consultant)
- Looking for a vendor, not a methodology partner
- Expects AI to replace humans, not amplify them
- Thinks governance is red tape, not infrastructure
- Won't invest time in understanding — just wants the output
- Treats the boundary map as a checkbox, not a living practice
