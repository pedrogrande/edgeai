### STAGE 1 — FAST TRUST: First Touchpoint (Awareness → "They understand my world")

This is where most AI companies fail. They lead with what AI *can do*. You need to lead with what *going wrong costs*.

**Method 1: The "What's At Stake" Content Strategy**

Instead of "AI can help your firm," publish content that names the specific professional risks of *unstructured* AI deployment — and does so with the precision of someone who understands their regulatory world:

- *"What happens when an AI agent drafts a compliance filing that's wrong — and your name is on it?"*
- *"The gap between 'the AI got it mostly right' and 'I'd put my name on this'"*
- *"Why 'the agent is 95% accurate' is meaningless without knowing WHICH 5%"*

These aren't scare tactics — they're **honest articulations of the risk your audience already carries but can't name precisely.** This is the specific language of professional indemnity, not generic AI hype. A tool vendor can't write this. A methodology provider who understands *why* the risk matters can. **Costly signal: you're naming the risk most AI vendors avoid.**

**Method 2: The 77% Challenge as Trust-First Entry Point**

Your 77% Challenge is already the strongest trust-first awareness mechanism in your plan. Here's how to weaponise it as a costly signal:

- **Make it the centrepiece, not a side offering.** It should be *the* thing people encounter when they first find you.
- **Publish the results.** Every complementarity analysis produces a one-page boundary map. Aggregate and publish anonymised patterns — "Here's what we found across 15 law firms." This is a **reputation system signal**: you're building a data asset that grows more valuable with every participant, and you're sharing the insight publicly. The next firm sees that 14 firms before them did this. That's coordination game logic — common knowledge enables trust.
- **Name the firms that participate.** If they'll allow it, a "Participated in the 77% Challenge" badge on their site and yours creates mutual visibility and social proof. They get a trust signal (they take AI governance seriously); you get a trust signal (real firms trusted you with this process).

**Method 3: Professional Association Entry — The Procedural Justice Channel**

Professional associations (Law Society of Victoria, CPA Australia) are **structural trust infrastructure** for your audience. They're where trust decisions are vetted and validated. Your strategy here should be:

- **Don't sell to them. Offer to them.** Propose a joint "Agent Governance Readiness" session for members — free, no pitch, just the 77% Challenge methodology and findings. You're not selling Future's Edge; you're contributing to the association's mission of protecting their members' professional standards.
- **Ask them what their members are worried about.** Before proposing anything, run a listening session. This is procedural justice in action: you're letting them shape the engagement, not arriving with a pre-packaged offer. The association will trust you because you trusted *them* with defining the problem.
- **Publish the findings collaboratively.** A co-branded paper with the Law Society is a **third-party verification signal** that no amount of self-published content can match. It says: "this isn't just Peter's idea — the professional body for lawyers in Victoria has looked at this."

**Method 4: The CAWDP Working Paper as Structural Trust Signal**

The open methodology isn't just a business model choice — it's the most powerful trust signal you possess. Here's why:

- **An open methodology is a costly signal** because you're giving away what most companies would gate. An untrustworthy methodology provider would hide the process and sell access. Publishing CAWDP under CC BY-SA 4.0 says: "I'm confident enough in my methodology that I want it scrutinised, improved, and challenged publicly."
- **It enables verification** — a sceptical professional can read the entire methodology, test it on their own workflow, and decide whether it holds up. They don't have to trust *you*; they can trust the *process*.
- **It creates coordination** — once multiple professionals in a network are using the same vocabulary (complementarity analysis, authority boundaries, verification levels, progressive autonomy), you've created a focal point. Coordination game logic: shared expectations enable trust without central authority.

**But here's the critical design choice: the working paper must NOT read like marketing.** It must read like what it is — a methodology paper with citations, limitations, and explicit acknowledgment of where CAWDP doesn't yet have evidence. Every place you say "we don't know yet" is a trust deposit. Every place you overclaim is a trust withdrawal.

### STAGE 2 — DEMONSTRATION: Early Engagement (They test you without risk)

**Method 5: The Boundary Map as Your "Proof Before Purchase" Mechanism**

The one-page boundary map from the 77% Challenge is your version of a free trial — but better, because it's *specific to their firm.* This is the key mechanism design insight:

- A generic free trial says "try our product." A boundary map says "here's exactly what agents should and shouldn't do in YOUR workflow, and here's where your professional risk sits."
- The map is valuable whether they hire you or not. That's the costly signal: you're delivering professional-grade insight *before* asking for commitment.
- It's also a **risk-reversal mechanism**: the firm takes no risk by doing the 77% Challenge. You're bearing all the time cost. They get the output regardless of whether they proceed.

**Method 6: Live Problem-Solving Workshops (Not Sales Workshops)**

Your half-day workshops ($495-795) should be structured as **demonstrations, not presentations.** The format that builds the most trust:

1. A firm brings a real workflow they want to automate
2. You run a live CAWDP decomposition in the room
3. They leave with a boundary map they co-created
4. They see the methodology work in real time on a real problem
5. The other firms in the room see it too (social proof + community)

This is public problem-solving — the costliest competence signal available. You're demonstrating the methodology in an uncontrolled environment where mistakes are visible. That's what makes it credible.

**Method 7: "Born-Agent-Native" Transparency**

You run your own operations on CAWDP. Show it. Publish your own complementarity analysis for your own workflows. Show where you use agents, where Peter and Rifadh make judgments, and what your verification levels are. This is **eating your own cooking** — the professional services equivalent of "skin in the game." It's also a structural trust signal: you've designed your own business around the methodology you're selling.

### STAGE 3 — RECIPROCITY: Relationship Deepening (They start to trust your motives)

**Method 8: The Anonymised Failure Report**

This is the single most powerful benevolence signal in your arsenal, and I suspect it's one you'll resist — which is exactly why it works:

Publish a report on where CAWDP *didn't work.* What workflows failed the complementarity analysis? Where did agents produce outputs that didn't pass verification? What did you get wrong in the 77% Challenge, and what did you learn?

This is a **costly signal of integrity** because:
- No AI vendor publishes failures. They publish wins.
- A professional services owner reads this and thinks: "This person tells the truth even when it costs them."
- It demonstrates that your verification methodology actually works — it caught the failures, and you're honest about them.
- It creates **procedural justice**: you hold yourself to the same standard you're asking clients to adopt.

**Method 9: Connect Firms to Each Other (Bridging Social Capital)**

When you've run the 77% Challenge with 10-15 firms, you're sitting on the most valuable asset in professional services: **a network of peers who are all navigating the same problem.** 

- Create a private, invitation-only roundtable for 77% Challenge participants. No Future's Edge branding. No pitch. Just: "You're all trying to figure out how to trust agents with professional work. Talk to each other."
- You're building **bridging social capital** — connecting firms that wouldn't normally share insights because they're competitors. This is enormously valuable to them and costs you nothing but facilitation.
- It's also a **lock-in mechanism** that doesn't feel like lock-in: the community becomes more valuable as it grows, and you're the node that connects it.

### STAGE 4 — INSTITUTIONALISATION: Structural Trust (They don't need to trust *you* — they trust the *system*)

**Method 10: The Agent Production Report as Category-Defining Infrastructure**

Your "Agent Production Report" — the annual benchmarking of agent trust across early adopters — is the mechanism that converts "Peter's methodology" into "industry infrastructure." Here's the trust architecture:

- **It's third-party-verified by design.** You're not rating yourself — you're rating the industry, and you're publishing the methodology for how you rate it.
- **It creates a coordination point.** Once the report exists, every professional services firm can benchmark themselves against it. "Are we above or below the industry standard for agent governance?" This creates common knowledge — and common knowledge is what makes coordination games solvable.
- **It makes your methodology the standard.** Not by declaration, but by being the measurement system everyone uses. Moody's didn't become authoritative by claiming authority — they became authoritative by being the rating system everyone referenced.

**Method 11: Professional Indemnity Insurer Partnership — Structural Trust at Scale**

This is your highest-leverage trust mechanism because it converts trust from *voluntary* to *structural*:

- If even ONE professional indemnity insurer starts requiring or recognising CAWDP-based agent governance documentation, **trust in your methodology is no longer a choice — it's a condition of insurance.**
- This is mechanism design: you're embedding trust in the institutional infrastructure, not relying on individual relationships.
- Start by offering to help an insurer understand agent risk in professional services. Run a 77% Challenge-style analysis of their exposure. Show them what "agent governance documentation" looks like. Again — give value first, ask for partnership second.

## 🗺️ YOUR AWARENESS SEQUENCE — TRUST-FIRST, NOT VOLUME-FIRST

Here's the order that builds trust accumulatively:

```
MONTH 1-2: PUBLISH THE SIGNALS
  → CAWDP working paper (open, rigorous, limitations stated)
  → 2-3 "What's At Stake" articles (professional-risk-specific, not AI-hype)
  → Your own complementarity analysis published publicly
  → 77% Challenge landing page: "We'll map your agent boundaries for free"

MONTH 2-4: BUILD THE EVIDENCE
  → Run 10-15 77% Challenges (free, named where possible)
  → Publish aggregated findings (anonymised patterns, named participants with consent)
  → Begin professional association conversations (listen first, propose second)
  → Publish first case study (imperfect, honest, real)

MONTH 4-6: CREATE THE FOCAL POINT
  → First paid workshops (live CAWDP decomposition format)
  → Professional association co-branded session
  → Failure report (where CAWDP didn't work)
  → Participant roundtable (peer community begins)
  → Start insurer conversation (offer analysis of their exposure, not a product)

MONTH 6+: INSTITUTIONALISE
  → Agent Production Report (first benchmarking)
  → Certification launch (Foundation level)
  → Academic co-authorship published
  → Community infrastructure live (GitHub, Discourse)
```

**The ordering is deliberate:** you cannot launch paid workshops without case studies. You cannot approach professional associations without published methodology. You cannot approach insurers without evidence. Each stage is a trust prerequisite for the next. **Speed is the enemy of trust-building.**
